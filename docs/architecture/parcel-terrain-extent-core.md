# Parcel and terrain extent Core contract

Date: 2026-09-30. Status: offline Core implementation evidence for Issue #60; this note makes no Revit runtime claim.

`ParcelExtentGeometry` is the opt-in input for a Revit parcel workflow. It carries a projected, unbuffered legal `PolygonalRegion`, a terrain-only horizontal margin, and an optional host-provided minimum legal edge length. Passing it as the final optional argument to `TerrainProcessingPipeline.RunAsync` leaves every existing CLI call unchanged.

The pipeline resolves a `TerrainExtentPlan` in the same local frame as its payload. Its legal region, local legal boundary, legal plane Z, terrain clip region, projected fetch envelope, clip results, and typed warnings are bounded Core values. The legal boundary comes from the original parcel's rings, holes, and polygons. `LegalBoundaryFactory` removes only exactly coincident vertices and vertices exactly between collinear neighbors; it rejects a separately supplied short edge rather than moving, snapping, or buffering it. The Revit host remains responsible for its API-specific loop validation.

For the opt-in path, southwest and centroid origins derive from the legal parcel's unbuffered envelope before clipping. Explicit origins remain exactly as supplied. Legal plane Z is the minimum valid elevation from the unsimplified legal-area clip. If that clip has no valid sample, `ParcelExtentPlanningException` names the pre-transaction failure; it never borrows an elevation from the terrain margin. The complete terrain extent, including a zero-margin legal parcel, must fit inside the elevation grid's finite cell-corner envelope before terrain clipping; otherwise the same named exception blocks creation.

The plan emits typed counts for legal multipart geometry, legal holes, topology changes introduced by buffering, and legal/terrain NODATA cells. NODATA stays absent data throughout; no elevation is synthesized for a legal plane or terrain sample.

Before acquisition, `ParcelFetchEnvelopePlanner.Build` accepts the unbuffered canonical WGS 84 `ParcelGeometryAoi` and the separate terrain margin. The grid's actual CRS is not known until the two existing source responses arrive, so this helper builds the same projected terrain buffer for every 40 already-verified NAD83-family UTM candidate (zones 10N through 19N and their four realizations). It inverse-transforms a boundary densified to five metres, including buffered ring arcs and each candidate terrain envelope, unions those WGS 84 samples, applies a ten-metre numerical allowance, then uses the established minimum fetch-side policy. It never selects a candidate CRS, makes another HTTP request, or retries a paid request; the post-grid complete-coverage guard remains authoritative. It rejects pre-buffered, projected, and other geographic inputs rather than inventing a geographic buffer or an unverified source transform. The WGS 84-to-NAD83 family transforms use the existing accepted ProjNET zero-datum-shift approximation; this is a fetch-coverage calculation, not a new geodetic-accuracy claim.

Offline evidence is in `TerrainProcessingPipelineTests` and `LegalBoundaryFactoryTests`. It covers unbuffered legal-boundary/world-coordinate invariance across southwest, centroid, and explicit origins; zero, one, three, and six-cell margins; and both fully retained and fixed point budgets. The explicit-origin cases prove that an explicit source Z changes the local legal plane Z while reconstructing the same legal world Z. It also covers multipart/hole and NODATA warning counts; partial-extent rejection at both zero and positive margin; no-valid-legal-sample failures; and lossless collinear cleanup/short-edge rejection. These synthetic checks do not observe a Revit toposolid, PropertyLine, or surface elevation. The planned Revit 2027 edge experiment and its measurement method remain required before any edge-quality or host-shape claim.

`ParcelFetchEnvelopePlannerTests` additionally checks zero and 200-metre margins at the public example site and a runtime-derived UTM zone-edge rectangle. For every supported candidate it independently rebuilds the terrain buffer and samples its inverse-transformed rings, rounded buffer arcs, and envelope edges at one-metre spacing; every sample is contained by the computed request.
